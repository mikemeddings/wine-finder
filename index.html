      Import a cellar from CellarTracker

      Sharing your cellar with the family? Just get the file from CellarTracker (steps below) and email it to the person who looks after the website. They do the rest. The family sees the wines and scores only: no prices, bin locations, notes or bottle counts.

      How to get the file from CellarTracker
      1. On a computer (not the phone app), sign in at cellartracker.com and open My Cellar.
      2. Switch to the Wines view (one row per wine).
      3. Press Export at the top right and choose CSV. Tick at least Wine, Vintage, Producer, Country, Region, Varietal, Quantity and the scores (your own and the community's), then export.
      4. Email the file, then save it on this computer.

      The buttons along the top

      Each button has its own colour, and the page you're on has a white ring around its button.

      • Find wines (gold): every wine in the stores and the family cellars, to narrow down and sort.
      • My cellars (orange): your own cellar and ratings.
      • Short List (green): wines you've picked for Claude to research. The number is how many are on it.
      • Comparison List (blue, with the scales): wines you're weighing up to buy, side by side.
      • Research (purple): look a wine up, add one by name, and the Sommelier & Food Pairing page for matching the two.
      • Restaurant (teal): help choosing from a wine list when you're out.
      • Help (pale blue): this page. Start over (white) clears every choice.

      Finding wines

      The Find wines page has three numbered areas, worked through in order:

      1. ① Choose your wines, the column on the left: first Find me a wine, the big quick buttons (colour, price, style…), then More filters below it for everything else (stores, country, grape, food, vintage…).
      2. ② Sort the list, the blue box at the top right: which wines come first, for example best rated or cheapest.
      3. ③ Your choices, the pink box above the wines: everything that's narrowing the list right now. Tap ✕ on any choice to drop it.

      Find me a wine, at the top of the left column, has big buttons: colour, price, body, tannin, “Fruity & ripe”, sweetness, where it's from, “Only well rated” and “Ready to drink now”. Tap ▴ Hide choices to fold the panel away (your choices stay, summed up in one line; your quick picks stay visible), and ▾ Show choices to open it again; each computer remembers how you left it. Tap one to choose it, tap it again to undo it; the list below changes as you go. In Body, Tannin and Sweetness you can choose more than one, for example Medium and Full-bodied for wines that are either. Tannin and “Fruity & ripe” are for reds, so they're hidden when you choose only White, Rosé or Sparkling. The number on each button is how many wines you'd get by tapping it, with your other choices; a dashed button would give none. If nothing matches, the page says which choices rule everything out and offers to show the wines without them. Fruity & ripe is for reds that taste ripe and fruity even though they're dry, like many Malbecs, Zinfandels and Australian Shiraz; Sweetness has four steps, Dry, Off-dry, Medium-sweet and Sweet, for sugar actually left in the wine; each button shows what it means in BC's sugar code and the LCBO's grams per litre. Body, tannin, fruitiness, sweetness and “ready now” are Claude's answer where Claude has scored the wine (“Get quick Claude scores (next 25)”), and otherwise an estimate from the grape, region and alcohol. “Only well rated” means 90 points or more from Claude, CellarTracker or the family, or on ZYN's own 90+ list.

      Quick picks: ⭐ Scored by Claude or the family keeps only wines that Claude, CellarTracker or a family member has scored, on top of your other choices; the number says how many that leaves. Tap it again to switch it off. Under the price buttons, a slider sets the lowest and highest price, and the count beside it changes as you move it. With a family cellar and a store both ticked, a cellar wine takes that store's price, so “Under $100” means wines the store sells for under $100; the heading says how many of the cellar's wines the store sells, and each store under Sold by shows that number too. Once you've chosen what you like, tap 💾 Save as my quick pick and give it a name: it becomes a button of your own, on this computer only. Tap ✕ beside it to remove it.

      Filters (on the left; on a phone, tap “Show filters”) narrow the list:

      • Search words: a name, producer, region or grape.
      • Sold by: BC Liquor – LDB, the Calgary stores BSW Liquor and ZYN (check shipping to BC), Lucullus and Weinshop365 in Switzerland (prices in Swiss francs as each shop shows them, Lucullus without VAT and Weinshop365 with it, plus an approximate Canadian-dollar price), and LCBO (Ontario). LCBO is a private family list: the first time you tick it, enter the unlock code from the family email (or click the link in that email). After that it stays on that browser.
      • Country (tick the ones you want; or press Leave out countries and tick the ones you don't want, such as USA, which then show an ✕), Type of wine, Grape (from the store's grape list, the name, or the region: Barolo counts as Nebbiolo), Food it goes with (an estimate from the wine's style; tick Include adventurous pairings for rule-breakers such as a soft red with salmon or tuna, each with a line of advice on the card), Vintage and Alcohol (sliders; wines without a vintage or alcohol figure drop out while you use them, and only BC Liquor and the LCBO give the alcohol), Price (two sliders, lowest and highest, with a live count) and Sweetness: Dry, Off-dry, Medium-sweet or Sweet, the same four steps as in Find me a wine (choosing in one place chooses in both). BC Liquor's sugar code (0–10) and the LCBO's grams of sugar a litre decide it where the store gives them; each wine card says which, for example “Off-dry (LCBO: 8 g/L sugar)” or “Dry (estimated)”. Untick Include wines whose sweetness is estimated to see only wines with a measured figure.

      The Your choices box sums up what you've chosen, one chip per choice; tap a chip's ✕ to drop just that one. Sort by puts the list in order. Best rated first (the usual order; ratings come from Claude, CellarTracker and the family, never from the stores) puts wines Claude has scored first (including a score given to the same wine at another store), then CellarTracker scores, then the family's own, then ZYN's 90+ list, then everything else, cheapest first. Other choices include price, name, country, vintage, alcohol, sweetness, Claude's score, my rating (on this computer), family members' ratings (real people only), CellarTracker score and “In the family's cellars first”, each either way round. The two then by boxes break ties: for example Country, then Best rated first, then Price low to high. CellarTracker score is the cellar owner's own score on CellarTracker, or the CellarTracker community score when they haven't given one.

      Each wine card shows the price, the store, the alcohol and sweetness, and any family ratings. Its buttons:

      • ＋ Add to Short List for Claude Research: puts the wine on your list for Claude (see topic 3).
      • Find it elsewhere: buttons to search other stores for this wine.
      • CellarTracker, Wine Enthusiast, Vivino: open those websites' reviews in a new tab.
      • ☆ Your rating: give the wine your own score out of 100.
      • 📖 Claude Research Results: appears when someone in the family has had Claude research this wine. Tap it to read the full write-up.

      Start over (top right) clears all the filters. It clears every page's choices: Find wines, the Sommelier & Food Pairing page, My cellars and the Dining out question (your saved restaurant picks stay). It asks before emptying your Short List for Claude, your Comparison List and your Food Pairings list.

      Where can I buy…?

      On the Research page (🔎 at the top), type the name of a wine someone recommended. Fewer words find more: the producer's name is usually enough. You'll see:

      • In the family's wines: the wine in a family cellar, or already researched by the family, with its ratings and a 📖 button. Check here before paying for a new Claude run.
      • On our store lists: where BC Liquor, BSW or ZYN sell it, and the price.
      • Or add it by name: puts it on the Short List for Claude even if no store has it.
      • Search other stores: buttons that open each store's own search (Vessel in Victoria, BSW and ZYN in Calgary, Everything Wine, Liberty, Marquis, the LCBO, and two Swiss shops).

      Food pairings on each wine

      Every wine card has a 🍽 Food pairings fold: tap ▸ Show to open it. It lists the classic pairings, with stars (★★★ the go-to, ★★ a common match, ★ pairs but less often chosen) and the dishes the pairing books name, then the adventurous pairings, the rule-breakers that sommeliers back, each with why it works and what to watch for. Where the LCBO's tasting note or Claude's research names a dish, that's shown first. The LCBO's own tasting note opens the same way.

      Sommelier & Food Pairing: matching wine and food

      Press Sommelier & Food Pairing (black and gold) at the top. It matches wine and food from six pairing books: Evan Goldstein's Perfect Pairings and Daring Pairings, Sid Goldstein's The Wine Lover's Cookbook, Dornenburg & Page's What to Drink with What You Eat, Robert Harrington's Food and Wine Pairing and The Food & Wine Guide to Perfect Pairings. Every suggestion says which book it's from, with the page. Recipes are given by title and page: the recipes themselves are in the books. First tick where you're buying under Buying from (the LCBO shows there once it's unlocked). Then choose one of three ways in:

      • 🍷 I have a bottle. Pick a wine from a ticked cellar, type or paste a name (or just a grape, such as Saperavi), or choose a country to see its wines grouped by grape. Then What have you got? asks what you're cooking: tap a food (★ is a classic match, ⚡ an adventurous one) or type what's in the fridge, and you get that wine with that food, with every recipe. More about this wine and its pairings holds the rest.
      • ⚖️ Wines on my Comparison List. The wines on your Comparison List, with country, price, store and grape. Tick some and press Pair the ticked wines, or Build the menu to send each bottle to the course it suits best.
      • 🍖 I have some food. Tap what's on the plate and choose how adventurous to be. With a cellar ticked, its wines appear at once in a table, what to open first at the top: click a heading to sort, or a wine to open it on that food. With a store ticked, you also get real bottles to buy, and See all in Find wines opens the full list.
      • 🧀 Plan a dinner. Leave the country on Any to choose from everything ticked, or choose one (for example Moldova). Say how many wines, which courses and, if you like, the main course. It picks a bottle for each course, says what to serve, and gives a recipe with Show all. Print this menu prints it; Ask Claude to polish this menu sends it to Claude for dishes and serving tips.

      In our cellars. Step 1 says where the bottles are: tick cellars and stores, and this computer remembers them. Ticking a cellar adds a Pick from the cellar tab: All the wines, Drink first, At their peak, The treasures, Old and wise, Light and easy, Big and bold, A touch of sweetness and Something adventurous, each a table you can sort and narrow (click a wine for its card), plus Surprise me. The tags 🔴 Drink first, 🟠 Drink soon, 🟢 Ready and ⏳ Not yet go by the owner's own drinking dates.

      Food Pairings list. Click a wine in a table and its card pops out. Add to Food Pairings list collects up to five wines, from any page, and See pairings shows what to cook with them together: a section for each wine, with the book's own recipes and same-style ones kept apart. ★ rates a wine with a food; the books don't rate recipes.

      Other wines for this dish. Open any recipe and, under the method, you'll find the bottles in the stores ticked that carry the grape the book pours with it, then bottles of grapes of the same style (marked as Claude's judgement, not the books'). Choose a country or a top price to narrow them; best rated first.

      The matches are a guide from the books, not a tasting: a wine's grape decides most of it. Blends and local grapes the books don't name are matched by the wine's style (body, tannin, sweetness).

      Research a wine with Claude

      Claude looks up each wine's reviews, background and history, suggests food to go with it, and gives its own score out of 100. Open it with Research at the top (add a wine by name, and “Where can I buy…?”) or Short List (the list itself and the Ask Claude buttons; the number is how many wines are on it).

      1. Add wines: type a name in the box, or press “＋ Add to Short List for Claude Research” on any wine.
      2. Ask Claude: press ② Ask Claude about these (or “rank these by reviews”). Claude opens in a new tab with the question already written; press Enter to send it. Claude's note “Use caution before running this prompt” appears for every question that arrives this way; this one is fine to send.
      3. Save the research: when Claude finishes, two clicks:

      The Wine Finder opens a box. When it shows ✅ Claude's write-up found, press Save (it asks your first name the first time). If it shows ❌, go back to Claude and click the copy icon: the box notices by itself.

      After saving, the ratings and Claude's whole write-up are kept on your computer and sent to the website keeper. Once published, everyone sees Claude's score and a 📖 Claude Research Results button on that wine.

      The Short List also has Copy as text, Email (you choose your email: Gmail, Outlook, the computer's own email program…), Print and Clear the list.

      Claude Quick Scores

      A quick, cheap way to get Claude's score for many wines at once, without the full research. Two ways: Get quick Claude scores (next 25) in the bar at the bottom scores the next 25 unscored wines in the list; or press ＋ Add Claude Quick Score (dark olive) on the wines you want, then Get Marked Quick Scores Now at the bottom. Claude opens with the question written; when it's done, click the save link at the end of its answer.

      The wine then shows a dark olive badge, Claude NN (Quick Score Only) ⓘ Details. Tap it for what Claude found, in plain words: body, tannin, sweetness, when to drink, fruit, finish, food and grapes. For reviews, background and history, use the full research on the Short List instead.

      🍽️ At a restaurant

      Photograph the wine list, and Claude picks the best wines on it for your meal and budget. It uses your own Claude, so there's no extra cost.

      1. Tap what you're eating (or type it), the colour, Bottle, By the glass or Either, and your budget. Slide Lowest and Highest, or tap a quick button. All the way right means no limit.
      2. Tap Open Claude. In Claude, tap ＋ or 📷, take a photo of the wine list (straight on), and send.
      3. At the end of Claude's answer, tap 🍷 Back to my Wine Finder with tonight's picks.

      Tonight's picks shows how many wines Claude read, its ranking, the ⛔ Wines to skip and the details of each pick. The skip tags mean: Overpriced a big markup, Wrong for your food, Weak reviews, Past its best too old.

      To see them again later: tap 🍷 Tonight's picks at the top (on the day), or Your recent picks on the restaurant page (the last 5). They're kept on that phone only.

      • Then tick stores under Sold by to see only the ones those stores sell. Each wine shows Sold at: with the store's price, or “not sold”.

      Grapes explained

      What each grape usually tastes like, in plain words. A guide only: where it's grown, the winemaker and the vintage all change it. If you like full body without bitter tannins, look for Malbec, Zinfandel, Grenache, Shiraz, Amarone and Carmenère, or tap 🍷 Smooth & bold in Find me a wine.

      Ratings explained

      All scores are out of 100.

      • Claude 96 Claude's score from a full research write-up, shared with the whole family. Claude 93 (Quick Score Only) (dark olive) is a score from “Get quick Claude scores (next 25)”: a score and the reviews it rests on, but no write-up.
      • Family 90 a family member's own score, shared with everyone.
      • Claude 94 (orange) Claude's score saved on this computer, not shared yet.
      • My rating 88 your own score on this computer (the ☆ button).
      • None of these come from the stores: BC Liquor and the others don't publish ratings.

      A rating marked “researched as …” or “rated as … (BC Liquor)” was given to the same wine under a slightly different name, often a different vintage. Critics' scores, with who gave them and for which vintage, are in Claude's write-ups (📖).

      Questions and fixes

      The Email button opened the wrong email program.
      It asks which email you use: choose yours (Gmail, Outlook.com…). For Thunderbird, make it this computer's email app in Windows Settings → Apps → Default apps.
      “Claude's write-up hasn't been copied yet.”
      Go back to the Claude tab and click the copy icon: two folders on top of each other, on the far left of the grey row under Claude's answer. Then come back.
      Claude's message box was empty (in the Claude app).
      The question is also copied: tap in the message box and paste.
      A second Wine Finder tab opened.
      The older one closes itself, or says it can be closed. Everything is saved.
      My restaurant picks disappeared.
      Tap 🍷 Tonight's picks at the top, or see Your recent picks on the restaurant page, on the same phone.
      The text is too small.
      On a computer hold Ctrl and press +; on a phone, spread two fingers.
      Are the prices current?
      Prices exclude tax and availability isn't guaranteed. The store lists are refreshed every three months; the date is at the bottom of each page.
